# Facilitator Script — Christ-Centered Unity (30 Minutes)

**Elders Quorum discussion | Sunday, October 18, 2026**  
**General Conference:** October 2026 (Sunday afternoon, October 4)  
**Speaker:** President Dieter F. Uchtdorf, Acting President of the Quorum of the Twelve Apostles  
**Address:** *Christ-Centered Unity*  
**Read / watch the complete official English talk (12:27):** https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/general-conference/2026/10/51uchtdorf?lang=eng  
**Lesson focus:** We become one through Jesus Christ, our covenants, and deliberate love for one another.

> **Source boundary:** This is an original facilitator script based on President Uchtdorf's talk, **not** a transcript of his address. All attributed quotations are brief and taken from the official source. The complete talk is available at the official Church link above. Teaching questions, transitions, and suggested testimony are facilitator-authored.

## Preparation (before quorum begins)

- Read the complete address at **[T1]** and review the four talk sections cited below.
- Have these scripture links ready: [Mosiah 18:8–9][S1], [Ephesians 2:14][S2], [4 Nephi 1:15–17][S3], and [Doctrine and Covenants 20:77][S4].
- On the board, write: **CHRIST → COVENANTS → BRIDGES → SACRAMENT → ONE ACTION**.
- Do not play the entire 12-minute video in a 30-minute discussion. If you use a clip, shorten discussion time accordingly.
- The 30-minute clock begins **after** opening prayer, announcements, and presidency business.

## 0:00–3:00 — Opening: What Holds Us Together? (3 minutes)

**SAY (facilitator):**

> Good morning, brethren. Today we're discussing President Dieter F. Uchtdorf's October 2026 General Conference message, *Christ-Centered Unity*. You can read or watch the complete talk here: https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/general-conference/2026/10/51uchtdorf?lang=eng
>
> President Uchtdorf describes baptism as more than an individual commitment. It also joins us to a people who walk the covenant path together. He reminds us that Jesus Christ, not our similar interests or opinions, is what brings us together. [T1]
>
> I'd love for this to be a discussion. We don't need perfect answers. We can learn from one another's experience and the scriptures.

**ASK:**

1. When you hear *unity*, what comes to mind?
2. Does being united require us to agree on everything? Why or why not?

**LISTEN** for two or three responses. Don't correct sincere personal experiences or insist on a predetermined answer.

**TRANSITION:**

> Let's begin with the commitments that connect us as disciples of Christ.

**SOURCE:** [T1, opening paragraphs and “The Unity Jesus Christ Offers”].

## 3:00–9:00 — Our Covenants Make Us a People (6 minutes)

**SCRIPTURE (invite one brother to read):** [Mosiah 18:8–9][S1].

**SAY:**

> President Uchtdorf remembers the baptisms of two great-grandchildren and his own baptism at age eight. He then teaches that baptism is both an individual ordinance and an entrance into a worldwide community of Saints. That matters because covenants affect how we treat other people, not only how we understand our personal relationship with God. [T1]

**ASK (use two or three):**

1. What words in Mosiah 18 describe what it means to be God's people?
2. What is the difference between *attending a quorum* and *belonging to a covenant community*?
3. Where might someone in our ward need a burden made lighter this week?

**FOLLOW-UP, if discussion is slow:**

> What is one simple thing a ministering brother can do that communicates, “You are not alone”?

**FACILITATOR CONNECTION:**

> The gospel asks us to become people who notice one another. We can have different backgrounds and still bear each other's burdens.

**SOURCE:** [T1, opening on baptism]; [S1]. These are discussion prompts, not direct quotations from President Uchtdorf.

## 9:00–16:00 — Christ Builds Bridges Across Differences (7 minutes)

**SCRIPTURE (invite two readers):** [Ephesians 2:14][S2], then [4 Nephi 1:15–17][S3].

**SAY:**

> In the world of the Savior's mortal ministry, people were divided by social, religious, and cultural boundaries. Jesus crossed those boundaries, served those who were excluded, and called very different people to follow Him. [T1, “Jesus Christ Unites Us”]
>
> President Uchtdorf also points to the Nephites and Lamanites, whose generations of conflict gave way to peace when they turned to Jesus Christ and His teachings. In Fourth Nephi, the absence of contention is attributed to God's love in the people's hearts. [T1; S3]

**SHORT DIRECT QUOTE FROM PRESIDENT UCHTDORF:**

> “The closer we come to Him, the closer we will come to each other” [T1, “The Unity Jesus Christ Offers”].

**ASK:**

1. What changed in the people of Fourth Nephi, and why?
2. How can following Christ change the way we perceive a brother whose background or opinions differ from ours?
3. What is a practical example of building a bridge rather than a wall in a family, at work, or in this quorum?

**OPTIONAL FOLLOW-UP:**

> Which is more difficult for us: noticing differences, or choosing patience when those differences become uncomfortable?

**KEEP IT CHRIST-CENTERED:** If discussion drifts toward partisan debates or criticism of individuals, gently ask, “How would following Jesus Christ shape our response?”

**SOURCE:** [T1, “Jesus Christ Unites Us” and “The Unity Jesus Christ Offers”]; [S2]; [S3].

## 16:00–23:00 — The Sacrament Unites Us (7 minutes)

**SCRIPTURE (invite one reader):** [Doctrine and Covenants 20:77][S4]. Optionally reference [Moroni 4:3][S5] if members have it open.

**SAY:**

> In the section *The Unifying Power of the Sacrament*, President Uchtdorf notes a remarkable feature of this ordinance: when we receive the sacrament, we are not individually addressed by name. We partake personally, but we renew our covenants in the company of other disciples. This can renew both our relationship with God and the way we relate to one another. [T1]
>
> Paul similarly teaches that many people partake of one bread as members of one body. [1 Corinthians 10:16–17][S6]

**ASK:**

1. What do you notice about the words of the sacrament prayer that could strengthen our unity?
2. How might participating together in the sacrament change the way we regard the person sitting across the chapel?
3. How can the sacrament help us make a fresh start when relationships have been strained?

**OPTIONAL PAUSE:** Allow ten quiet seconds for reflection before the second question.

**FACILITATOR CONNECTION:**

> Being united does not mean pretending disagreements or hurt never happened. With the Savior's help, we can become more humble, compassionate, and willing to take appropriate steps toward peace.

**SOURCE:** [T1, “The Unifying Power of the Sacrament”]; [S4]; [S5]; [S6]. Last paragraph is a facilitator application, not an attributed quote.

## 23:00–27:00 — Turn to a Neighbor: One Christlike Action (4 minutes)

**SAY:**

> President Uchtdorf concludes by inviting everyone to participate in the unity being built around Jesus Christ. His message emphasizes making people feel welcome and seeking bridges instead of walls. [T1, “Blessings of Unity in Christ”]
>
> Take two minutes with the person next to you. Think of one specific action you can take in the coming week to help another person feel included, valued, or supported.

**PAIR QUESTION (2 minutes):**

> What is one specific thing we could do this week to strengthen Christ-centered unity?

**DEBRIEF (2 minutes):** Invite one or two volunteers to share **an action**, not anyone else's private circumstances.

**EXAMPLES only if needed:** Reach out to an absent brother; listen patiently to a family member; welcome someone unfamiliar; follow through on a ministering visit; offer appropriate help to a neighbor.

**SOURCE:** [T1, “Blessings of Unity in Christ”]; applications are facilitator-authored.

## 27:00–30:00 — Invitation and Testimony (3 minutes)

**SAY:**

> Brethren, as we finish, I invite each of us to prayerfully identify **one person** and take **one Christlike action** this week. Let it be something small but sincere. We can build unity now, not merely hope for it someday. [T1, “The Unity Jesus Christ Offers”]
>
> If you feel comfortable, quietly make a commitment to yourself: Who will I reach out to, and what will I do?

**PAUSE 10 seconds.**

**SUGGESTED TESTIMONY — personalize; do not claim experiences you haven't had:**

> I am grateful that Jesus Christ invites each of us to come unto Him and to walk with one another along the covenant path. I believe He can change our hearts, help us look beyond differences, and teach us to serve one another. I know that as we turn toward Him, our homes, our quorum, and our ward can become more united. In the name of Jesus Christ, amen.

**SOURCE:** [T1, “The Unity Jesus Christ Offers” and “Blessings of Unity in Christ”]; [Matthew 18:20][S7] is an optional closing scripture. Testimony wording is an original draft for the facilitator.
