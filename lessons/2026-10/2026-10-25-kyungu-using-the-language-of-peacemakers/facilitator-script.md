# Elders Quorum — Facilitator Script

## Using the Language of Peacemakers

**Lesson Date:** Sunday, October 25, 2026  
**General Conference:** October 2026 — Sunday Morning Session  
**Speaker:** Elder Alfred Kyungu, Of the Seventy  
**Official Talk:** [Using the Language of Peacemakers](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/general-conference/2026/10/47kyungu?lang=eng)  
**Duration:** 30 minutes  
**Format:** Scripture-centered, discussion-based Elders Quorum lesson

### Lesson Objective

Help quorum members recognize that peacemaking is an essential expression of discipleship to Jesus Christ, identify language that harms or heals relationships, and commit to one specific change in how they communicate during the coming week.

**Central Question:**

> How can we become more like Jesus Christ by changing the words we choose, especially when conversations become difficult?

**Desired Outcomes**

By the end of the discussion, quorum members should be able to:

1. Explain why the Savior cares about the words we speak.
2. Identify the difference between contentious and Christlike language.
3. Understand how a change of heart produces a change in speech.
4. Practice replacing a harmful expression with a peacemaking alternative.
5. Choose one realistic action to apply during the coming week.

### Source and Quotation Conventions

- **SPEAKER QUOTE:** Exact words spoken or written by Elder Alfred Kyungu in the official October 2026 talk.
- **TALK PARAPHRASE:** A summary of Elder Kyungu's teachings, not his exact words.
- **SCRIPTURE:** A passage or reference from the standard works.
- **SAY:** Suggested facilitator-created teaching language, not a quotation from the speaker.
- **ASK:** Discussion question created for this lesson.
- **SOURCE:** Official talk or scripture supporting the teaching point.

## Preparation Checklist

### Before Sunday

- [ ] Read Elder Kyungu's entire talk prayerfully.
- [ ] Review Matthew 12:34–37, Ephesians 4:29, Alma 24:19, and John 14:27.
- [ ] Review Colossians 4:6 and 3 Nephi 11:29–30 as optional supporting scriptures.
- [ ] Prepare to display the central question.
- [ ] Have the participant handout available, printed or digitally.
- [ ] Choose a member to read Ephesians 4:29 and another to read Alma 24:19, if appropriate.
- [ ] Prepare two or three realistic examples of difficult conversations without identifying anyone.
- [ ] Consider one personal experience of choosing a more peaceful response that can be shared without exposing another person's private information.
- [ ] Pray for the Holy Ghost to guide the discussion.

### Teaching Principles

**Keep Jesus Christ at the center.** This lesson is about becoming His disciples, not merely improving conversational technique.

**Invite participation.** Ask questions that encourage reflection rather than simply checking factual recall.

**Protect privacy.** Do not invite members to describe identifiable family conflicts, confidential conversations, or grievances involving other quorum members.

**Allow silence.** Give members time to think before answering.

**Avoid forced disclosure.** Members may reflect privately rather than share sensitive experiences.

**Preserve the distinction between peace and avoidance.** Christlike speech can be honest, direct, and firm. Peacemaking does not require tolerating abuse, surrendering appropriate boundaries, or pretending that serious problems do not exist.

**Manage time.** Prioritize the scriptures, the central question, and the final invitation over covering every possible example.

## SECTION 1 — OPENING

### 0:00–4:00 | Words Can Wound or Heal

**Purpose:** Invite members to recognize the spiritual and relational influence of everyday language.

### SAY — Opening Introduction (1 minute)

"Brethren, today we are discussing Elder Alfred Kyungu's October 2026 General Conference message, *Using the Language of Peacemakers.*

"Every day, we use words at home, at work, at church, and in our conversations with others.

"Sometimes a few words can strengthen someone's confidence. Other times, a few careless words can damage a relationship.

"The question today is not simply whether we speak politely. It is whether our words reflect our commitment to follow Jesus Christ."

### SPEAKER QUOTE

> "We must choose language that strengthens confidence and preserves relationships."

— Elder Alfred Kyungu

### ASK — Opening Discussion (2 minutes)

**Primary question:**

"Why do you think the words we use can have such a lasting influence on relationships?"

**Possible follow-up questions:**

- "What makes a simple expression of appreciation so powerful?"
- "Why can words spoken in frustration be difficult to forget?"
- "What difference does it make when someone feels genuinely heard?"

Invite two or three brief responses.

### SAY — Transition (1 minute)

"Elder Kyungu teaches that choosing peaceful language is not just a matter of good manners. It is part of following Jesus Christ.

"To understand why, let's begin with what the Savior taught about the relationship between our hearts and our words."

### SOURCE

- [Elder Alfred Kyungu, *Using the Language of Peacemakers*](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/general-conference/2026/10/47kyungu?lang=eng)

## SECTION 2 — MAIN DOCTRINE

### 4:00–12:00 | Our Words Reflect Our Discipleship

**Purpose:** Teach that Christlike communication begins with the heart and should build up others.

### SAY — Introduce the Doctrine (1 minute)

"Elder Kyungu reminds us that the Savior and His Apostles repeatedly taught the importance of our speech.

"Jesus Christ taught that our words matter before God, and the Apostle Paul taught that our words should strengthen those who hear them.

"This makes communication a spiritual responsibility."

### SCRIPTURE 1 — Ephesians 4:29 (2 minutes)

Invite a member to read aloud:

> "Let no corrupt communication proceed out of your mouth, but that which is good to the use of edifying, that it may minister grace unto the hearers."

— Ephesians 4:29, KJV

[Read Ephesians 4:29 — Official Scripture](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/eph/4?lang=eng&id=p29#p29)

### ASK — Scripture Discussion (3 minutes)

**Primary question:**

"What does it mean for our words to 'minister grace' to someone?"

**Follow-up questions:**

- "How is speaking to build someone up different from simply avoiding criticism?"
- "Can we disagree with someone and still speak in a way that edifies?"
- "What might change in our homes or workplaces if we made edification our first goal?"

**Teaching insight:**

Edifying language is not necessarily language that agrees with everything another person says.

It is language that communicates truth while respecting the person's dignity.

A difficult truth can be spoken with patience, humility, and love.

### SCRIPTURE — Matthew 12:34–37 (1 minute)

Introduce the Savior's teaching:

> "For out of the abundance of the heart the mouth speaketh."

— Matthew 12:34, KJV

[Read Matthew 12:34–37 — Official Scripture](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/matt/12?lang=eng&id=p34-p37#p34)

Explain that Jesus also taught in verses 36–37 that we are accountable for our words.

### ASK — Heart and Speech (1 minute)

"What does this teach us about the relationship between our spiritual condition and the way we speak?"

If helpful, ask:

"Why might changing our hearts be more lasting than simply memorizing polite expressions?"

### TALK PARAPHRASE

Elder Kyungu teaches that we can deliberately identify expressions that cause conflict and replace them with words that promote peace. He also teaches that filling our hearts with the doctrine of Christ can transform the way we communicate.

### SAY — Transition

"These scriptures teach us that our words reveal something about our hearts.

"But Elder Kyungu goes further. He invites us not only to avoid hurtful language but to give it up deliberately.

"That brings us to a powerful example from the Book of Mormon."

## SECTION 3 — SECOND SCRIPTURE AND DISCUSSION

### 12:00–20:00 | Burying the Language of Contention

**Purpose:** Connect the converted Anti-Nephi-Lehies' covenant of peace with our personal decision to abandon contentious expressions.

### SAY — Introduce the Book of Mormon Example (1 minute)

"In Alma chapter 24, we read about a people who experienced a profound conversion to the Lord.

"They made a solemn commitment to abandon violence and buried their weapons of war.

"Elder Kyungu uses their example to teach an important principle about the words we choose."

### SCRIPTURE 2 — Alma 24:19 (2 minutes)

Invite a member to read Alma 24:19.

Ask members to listen for the relationship between conversion, peace, and the decision to abandon former behavior.

[Read Alma 24:19 — Official Scripture](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bofm/alma/24?lang=eng&id=p19#p19)

### SPEAKER QUOTE

> "we too can bury the language of contention for peace."

— Elder Alfred Kyungu

### SAY — Explain the Analogy (1 minute)

"The Anti-Nephi-Lehies did not simply promise to use their weapons more carefully. They buried them as an expression of their conversion.

"Elder Kyungu invites us to consider whether there are words or expressions we should stop using altogether.

"Their literal weapons were different from our words, but the principle of deliberately abandoning harmful behavior applies to us."

### ASK — Main Discussion (3 minutes)

**Primary question:**

"If we were to bury our own 'weapons of contention,' what kinds of words or habits might we need to give up?"

Possible responses include:

- Insults and name-calling.
- Sarcasm intended to humiliate.
- Accusations beginning with "You always" or "You never."
- Dismissing another person's feelings.
- Raising our voices to intimidate.
- Speaking in anger before understanding.
- Using silence as punishment rather than taking a healthy pause.

**Follow-up questions:**

"Why is it sometimes difficult to give up these habits?"

"What can help us change when a harmful expression has become automatic?"

### SCRIPTURE CONNECTION — 3 Nephi 11:29–30 (1 minute)

Briefly introduce the resurrected Savior's teaching that contention is not of Him and that such things should be done away.

[Read 3 Nephi 11:29–30 — Official Scripture](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bofm/3-ne/11?lang=eng&id=p29-p30#p29)

### SAY — Doctrinal Clarification

"The Savior's commandment to reject contention does not mean we must abandon truth, ignore injustice, or avoid necessary conversations.

"It means we should seek to handle disagreement without contempt, humiliation, or hostility.

"We can stand firmly for truth and still treat others as children of God."

## SECTION 4 — PRACTICAL APPLICATION

### 20:00–27:00 | Practice Speaking as Peacemakers

**Purpose:** Help quorum members translate gospel principles into specific changes in everyday communication.

### SAY — Introduce the Practice (1 minute)

"Elder Kyungu does something very practical in his talk.

"He identifies expressions that damage relationships and offers alternatives that can encourage understanding and peace.

"Let's practice applying that principle to ordinary situations."

### TALK PARAPHRASE

Elder Kyungu contrasts humiliating and discouraging expressions with words of appreciation, respect, patience, apology, forgiveness, reassurance, and reconciliation.

His message invites deliberate replacement of contentious language rather than merely suppressing anger.

### ACTIVITY — Replace the Expression (3 minutes)

Display or read the following facilitator-created examples.

| Contentious expression | Possible peacemaking replacement |
|---|---|
| "You never listen." | "I want us to understand each other. Can we talk?" |
| "This is all your fault." | "Let's understand what happened and what we can do next." |
| "You're wrong." | "I see this differently. May I explain my perspective?" |
| "Just stop talking." | "I'm feeling overwhelmed. Can we pause and continue later?" |
| "You always make mistakes." | "Let's look at what went wrong and how to improve it." |

**Facilitator note:** These replacement statements are original lesson examples, not quotations from Elder Kyungu.

### ASK — Interactive Practice

Choose one or two examples.

"How would you rephrase this statement in a way that is truthful, respectful, and constructive?"

Invite members to suggest alternatives.

Then ask:

"What changes when we replace accusation with an invitation to understand?"

### SCRIPTURE CONNECTION — Colossians 4:6 (1 minute)

The Apostle Paul counseled:

> "Let your speech be alway with grace"

— Colossians 4:6, KJV (excerpt)

[Read Colossians 4:6 — Official Scripture](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/col/4?lang=eng&id=p6#p6)

### ASK — Personal Reflection (1 minute)

"Where might the Lord be inviting you to use more gracious language this week?"

Offer possible areas for private reflection:

- At home.
- At work.
- In quorum or ward service.
- In a difficult conversation.
- In a text message or online discussion.
- In the way we speak to ourselves.

Do not ask members to disclose their personal circumstances.

### SAY — The One-Week Challenge (1 minute)

"I invite each of us to choose one expression or communication habit that we want to leave behind this week.

"Then choose one peacemaking expression to practice in its place.

"Before responding in a difficult moment, we can ask ourselves:

"'Will these words bring me and the other person closer to the Savior, or further away from His peace?'"

### APPLICATION INVITATION

**Bury One Phrase. Plant One Phrase.**

During the coming seven days:

1. **Identify** one phrase or habit that tends to create contention.
2. **Replace** it with a truthful, respectful, Christlike expression.
3. **Practice** the replacement in at least one real conversation.
4. **Reflect** each evening on whether your words strengthened peace.
5. **Repent and begin again** if you fall short.

Optional private reflection:

"What changed in my heart when I changed my words?"

## SECTION 5 — CLOSING

### 27:00–30:00 | The Savior Gives Us His Peace

**Purpose:** Connect the lesson to Jesus Christ's promise of peace and extend a final invitation.

### SCRIPTURE — John 14:27 (1 minute)

Read the Savior's words:

> "Peace I leave with you, my peace I give unto you"

— John 14:27, KJV (excerpt)

[Read John 14:27 — Official Scripture](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/john/14?lang=eng&id=p27#p27)

### SAY — Closing Summary

"Today we have learned three important principles.

"First, our words matter to the Savior because they reflect what is in our hearts.

"Second, disciples of Jesus Christ can deliberately bury the language of contention.

"Third, peacemaking is something we practice through small, intentional choices every day.

"The peace Jesus Christ offers is not dependent on everyone agreeing with us. We can seek His peace even when circumstances are difficult."

### SPEAKER QUOTE

> "This peace is real. It is powerful"

— Elder Alfred Kyungu

### ASK — Final Invitation

"What is one word, phrase, or communication habit you feel inspired to change this week?"

Allow a brief moment of silent reflection.

Do not require public responses.

### SAY — Weekly Invitation

"I invite you to bury one contentious phrase and replace it with one peacemaking phrase this week.

"Ask Heavenly Father to help you recognize the moment when you need to make that choice.

"Then act on the prompting."

### TESTIMONY DRAFT

*The following is suggested facilitator language, not a statement attributed to Elder Kyungu. Adapt it to express your own sincere testimony.*

"Brethren, I believe that Jesus Christ is the Prince of Peace.

"He has the power to change not only our circumstances but also our hearts.

"As we turn to Him, repent, and make a sincere effort to speak with greater kindness and respect, we can become more like Him.

"I believe that even small changes in our words can bless our families, strengthen our quorum, and help others feel the love of the Savior.

"I am grateful for the peace that Jesus Christ offers us and for His example of truth spoken in love.

"In the name of Jesus Christ, amen."

# Facilitator's Additional Notes

## If Discussion Becomes Too Quiet

Ask one of these questions:

- "What is one phrase that can help de-escalate a difficult conversation?"
- "Why is saying 'I'm sorry' sometimes more difficult than explaining why we were right?"
- "How can we listen in a way that helps another person feel respected?"
- "What role does humility play in peacemaking?"

## If Discussion Becomes Too Long

Use this transition:

"Thank you for those insights. To make sure we leave time to apply this principle, let's move to one specific action we can practice this week."

## If Members Disagree

Model the principle being taught.

Say:

"Those are different perspectives, and I appreciate the thoughtful contributions. How can we discuss differences in a way that demonstrates the language of peacemakers?"

## If a Sensitive Conflict Is Raised

Avoid inviting details or judging the individuals involved.

Say:

"Thank you for sharing that this can be difficult. We don't need to discuss private circumstances here. The principle we can focus on is how to communicate truthfully and respectfully while maintaining appropriate boundaries."

## If Time Is Running Short

Prioritize these four essentials:

1. Ephesians 4:29 — Words should edify.
2. Alma 24:19 — Bury the language of contention.
3. One practical replacement exercise.
4. John 14:27 — Christ offers His peace.

## Optional Deeper Doctrinal Connections

**Proverbs 6:16–19:** The Lord condemns lying and sowing discord.

**Matthew 12:36–37:** Our words carry moral accountability.

**Colossians 4:6:** Speech should be gracious and wise.

**3 Nephi 11:29–30:** The Savior teaches us to do away with contention.

**Mosiah 4:30:** Watch our thoughts, words, and deeds.

**Doctrine and Covenants 121:41–43:** Righteous influence is exercised through persuasion, long-suffering, gentleness, meekness, love, and appropriate correction.

These are optional extensions, not required portions of the 30-minute lesson.
