# Elders Quorum — Facilitator Script

## Love Like Jesus

**Lesson Date:** Sunday, November 1, 2026  
**General Conference:** October 2026 — Saturday Afternoon Session  
**Speaker:** Elder Ronald A. Rasband, Quorum of the Twelve Apostles  
**Official Talk:** [Love Like Jesus](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/general-conference/2026/10/210rasband?lang=eng)  
**Duration:** 30 minutes  
**Format:** Scripture-centered, discussion-based Elders Quorum lesson

### Lesson Objective

Help quorum members understand how Jesus Christ's perfect love guides their relationships with people who make choices they cannot control, and how faith in His Atonement allows them to love without coercion, guilt, or despair.

**Central Question:**

> How can we love someone as Jesus Christ does when we cannot change that person's choices?

**Desired Outcomes**

By the end, quorum members should be able to:

1. Explain how Christlike love respects agency while remaining compassionate and engaged.
2. Identify lessons from Alma the Elder's prayers and the father of the prodigal son.
3. Recognize that the Savior carries burdens beyond our ability to resolve.
4. Choose one specific way to express Christlike love during the coming week.

### Source and Quotation Conventions

- **SPEAKER QUOTE:** Exact, brief wording from Elder Rasband's October 2026 address.
- **TALK PARAPHRASE:** Faithful summary of Elder Rasband's message, not a quotation.
- **SCRIPTURE:** Biblical or Book of Mormon passage, with official Church scripture link.
- **SAY:** Suggested words for the facilitator; not attributed to Elder Rasband.
- **ASK:** Discussion question for the quorum; not attributed to Elder Rasband.
- **SOURCE:** Identifies the supporting talk or scripture passage.

Scriptures should be read from the official text. Participants are not asked to reveal private family situations or personal histories.

# 30-Minute Teaching Plan

| Time | Section | Length |
|---|---|---|
| 0–3 | Opening and central question | 3m |
| 3–10 | Main doctrine: Love and agency | 7m |
| 10–18 | Scripture: Alma and faithful prayer | 8m |
| 18–24 | Scripture: The prodigal son | 6m |
| 24–28 | Application and invitation | 4m |
| 28–30 | Testimony and closing | 2m |

**Total: 30 minutes**

---

## SECTION 1 — OPENING

### 0:00–3:00 | What does it mean to love like Jesus?

**SAY (0:00–1:00)**

"Today we are discussing Elder Ronald A. Rasband's talk, Love Like Jesus.

"His message addresses something many people experience: loving someone deeply while being unable to control that person's choices.

"Rather than asking how we can make another person change, I want us to consider what the Savior asks of us."

**SPEAKER QUOTE**

> "We cannot change their hearts, but He can."

— Elder Ronald A. Rasband

**SOURCE:** Rasband, *Love Like Jesus*, paragraph beginning "Listen carefully."

**ASK (1:00–3:00)**

"What is the difference between loving someone and trying to control their choices?"

Invite two or three brief responses.

If discussion is slow, ask:

"Can we disagree with someone's choices and still show genuine Christlike love?"

**TRANSITION**

"To answer that, let's begin with the commandments Jesus Himself identified as the greatest."

---

## SECTION 2 — MAIN DOCTRINE

### 3:00–10:00 | Love, commandments, and agency

**Scripture reading — Matthew 22:36–40**

**SCRIPTURE:** Matthew 22:36–40

Ask someone to read verses 37–39 aloud.

**SAY (3:00–5:00)**

"Jesus placed love for God and love for our neighbor at the center of His commandments. These are not competing duties. Loving God shapes how we treat other people."

**TALK PARAPHRASE**

Elder Rasband teaches that love means taking an interest in others' lives, successes, and challenges. It includes being present, lifting with faith, and seeking the calming influence of the Holy Ghost. Love does not require endorsing every decision someone makes.

**SPEAKER QUOTE**

> "Love is not stepping back or sanctioning others' decisions and actions."

— Elder Ronald A. Rasband

**SOURCE:** Rasband, paragraph beginning "Love is not stepping back."

**ASK (5:00–8:00)**

"What might it look like to remain loving without approving of a harmful or unwise decision?"

Follow-ups:

"How can we express concern without lecturing or arguing?"
"What changes when we respect another person's agency?"
"How do we keep appropriate boundaries while showing compassion?"

Allow discussion. Emphasize principles rather than inviting personal disclosures.

**SAY (8:00–10:00)**

"Christlike love is neither control nor indifference. We can hold to gospel principles, respect agency, and still communicate that a person matters to us.

"Sometimes the most faithful action is to stop trying to win an argument and start listening."

**SOURCE:** Matthew 22:36–40; Rasband, paragraphs on agency, the two great commandments, and the meaning of love.

**TRANSITION**

"One of the strongest scriptural examples of loving someone whose choices we cannot control comes from Alma the Elder."

---

## SECTION 3 — SECOND SCRIPTURE AND DISCUSSION

### 10:00–18:00 | Alma the Elder and the power of prayer

**Scripture reading — Mosiah 27 and Alma 36**

**SCRIPTURE:** Mosiah 27:8–16; Alma 36:17–20

**SAY (10:00–11:00)**

"Alma the Younger actively opposed the Church. His father could not compel him to believe. Yet Alma the Elder continued to exercise faith in God."

Ask one participant to read Mosiah 27:14, then another to read Alma 36:17–20.

**ASK (11:00–14:00)**

"What do these passages teach us about the relationship between a parent's prayers, God's power, and a person's own decision to repent?"

Possible follow-ups:

"Who ultimately turned Alma the Younger's heart toward Jesus Christ?"
"Why is it important that Alma himself remembered Christ and cried for mercy?"

**TALK PARAPHRASE**

Elder Rasband uses Alma's conversion to encourage faithful prayer for loved ones. He points to the Savior's power to bring about spiritual change that others cannot produce through pressure or persuasion.

**SPEAKER QUOTE**

> "Love is expressed in prayer."

— Elder Ronald A. Rasband

**SOURCE:** Rasband, paragraph introducing Alma the Younger.

**SAY (14:00–16:00)**

"Notice that the account centers on Jesus Christ's redeeming power. Alma's father prayed, but the son personally turned to the Savior.

"Prayer is an expression of faith and love, not a means of taking away another person's agency. Nor should we assume that every prayer will be answered in the same way or on the same timetable."

**ASK (16:00–18:00)**

"How might our prayers change if we focused less on controlling an outcome and more on seeking the Savior's help for everyone involved?"

Allow two or three responses.

**SOURCE:** Mosiah 27:14; Alma 36:17–20; Rasband's discussion of Alma and prayer.

**TRANSITION**

"Alma shows the power of prayer and personal repentance. Jesus' parable of the prodigal son shows us what welcoming love looks like."

---

## SECTION 4 — THIRD SCRIPTURE AND DISCUSSION

### 18:00–24:00 | The prodigal son and welcoming love

**Scripture reading — Luke 15:11–24**

**SCRIPTURE:** Luke 15:11–24

Ask a participant to read verses 17–20, and another to read verses 21–24.

**SAY (18:00–20:00)**

"The younger son chose to leave. The father did not remove his ability to choose. When the son returned, the father responded with compassion.

"The parable invites us to consider what kind of relationship makes it possible for someone to come home."

**SPEAKER QUOTE**

> "He knew where to find it."

— Elder Ronald A. Rasband

**SOURCE:** Rasband, paragraph beginning "What brought him back?"

**ASK (20:00–23:00)**

"What did the father do that made his love unmistakable?"

Follow-ups:

"How did the father respond when he saw his son returning?"
"What might we learn from the father about making room for repentance and reconciliation?"
"How can we keep a door open without ignoring necessary boundaries or consequences?"

**SAY (23:00–24:00)**

"Love does not eliminate agency or consequences. But we can avoid turning another person's mistakes into their permanent identity.

"We can seek to be people who are safe to approach, willing to listen, and ready to encourage sincere change."

**SOURCE:** Luke 15:11–24; Rasband's discussion of the prodigal son.

**TRANSITION**

"Elder Rasband concludes with three suggestions. Let's turn those into one action we can take this week."

---

## SECTION 5 — PRACTICAL APPLICATION

### 24:00–28:00 | Three invitations

**TALK PARAPHRASE**

Elder Rasband closes with three recommendations:

1. Release guilt and continue loving rather than surrendering to despair.
2. Place the value of a person above the urgency of solving a problem.
3. Remember Jesus Christ, study His teachings, and pray with faith.

**SOURCE:** Rasband, three numbered closing suggestions.

**SCRIPTURE:** Matthew 11:28–30

Ask a participant to read verse 28.

**SPEAKER QUOTE**

> "Trust Him. He's got this!"

— Elder Ronald A. Rasband

**SOURCE:** Rasband, concluding paragraph.

**SAY (24:00–26:00)**

"The Savior does not ask us to carry every burden alone. We can do our part in love while trusting Him with what lies beyond our control.

"That trust does not guarantee a particular outcome this week. It means we turn to the One who understands every person and every burden."

**ASK (26:00–27:00)**

"Which of Elder Rasband's three suggestions would make the greatest difference in the way we treat people this week?"

Invite one or two brief responses.

**WEEKLY INVITATION (27:00–28:00)**

"Choose one person you can treat with more Christlike love this week. Without trying to control the person's response, do one specific thing: listen with patience, offer appropriate help, express appreciation, or pray sincerely for that person.

"Then pray for the Savior to change your own heart as well."

Allow a few seconds of silent reflection.

---

## SECTION 6 — CLOSING

### 28:00–30:00 | Closing testimony and invitation

**Closing recap**

**SAY (28:00–29:00)**

"Today we have seen three principles.

"First, love is the center of the Savior's commandments.

"Second, prayer and faith invite God's help where our own influence is limited.

"Third, the father's welcome in the parable of the prodigal son reminds us that compassion and repentance belong together.

"Jesus Christ is the center of all three."

**Suggested testimony draft**

**SAY (29:00–30:00)**

"I testify that Jesus Christ is our Savior and Redeemer. His love is greater than our mistakes, fears, and limitations.

"I believe that as we turn to Him, He can help us become more patient, more compassionate, and more faithful in our relationships.

"We cannot make every choice for those we love, but we can choose to follow Jesus Christ and reflect His love.

"I invite each of us to act on one prompting this week and trust the Lord with what we cannot control.

"In the name of Jesus Christ, amen."

*Personalize the testimony so it reflects what you sincerely believe.*
