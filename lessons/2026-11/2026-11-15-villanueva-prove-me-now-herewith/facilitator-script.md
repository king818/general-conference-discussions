# Facilitator Script — Prove Me Now Herewith (30 Minutes)

**Elders Quorum discussion | Sunday, November 15, 2026**  
**General Conference:** October 2026, Sunday Afternoon Session  
**Speaker:** Elder Moisés Villanueva, General Authority Seventy  
**Address:** *Prove Me Now Herewith*  
**Read / watch the complete official English talk:** https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/general-conference/2026/10/53villanueva?lang=eng  
**Lesson objective:** Help quorum members strengthen their faith in Heavenly Father's promises, recognize significant but subtle blessings from living the law of tithing, and choose one practical act of faithful obedience and gratitude this week.

**Central question:** How can we trust the Lord's promises when His blessings do not come in the way or at the time we expect?

## Preparation (before quorum begins)

- Prayerfully read Elder Villanueva's entire address.
- Read Malachi 3:8–12, Doctrine and Covenants 130:20–21, and 1 Kings 17:8–16.
- Prepare one personal example of a quiet blessing, without suggesting every favorable event proves a particular cause.
- Have the scripture passages ready on a phone or printed sheet.
- Prepare the participant handout.
- Invite participation without requiring anyone to reveal financial circumstances or tithing status.
- Prepare to keep the lesson focused on faith in Jesus Christ rather than financial outcomes.
- Facilitator safeguard: Do not imply that unemployment, illness, poverty, or delayed blessings establish someone's lack of faith or obedience. Avoid asking members to disclose tithing payments, household income, or financial hardship.

## 0:00–4:00 — Opening: Faith and Expectations (4 minutes)

**SAY:**

> Brethren, today we will discuss Elder Moisés Villanueva's message, 'Prove Me Now Herewith.'
>
> The Lord promises blessings when we obey His commandments. Yet sometimes we obey faithfully and the result we hoped for does not come.
>
> Today I invite us to consider not only whether we trust God's promises, but also how we recognize His hand when His answer is different from our expectations.

**SHORT DIRECT QUOTE FROM ELDER VILLANUEVA:**

> "Sometimes we expect the Lord's blessings to come the way we would wish, rather than in His own way and timing." [T1]

**ASK:**

1. When have you learned something important because an answer to prayer or a hoped-for blessing came differently than expected?

**LISTEN:** Allow approximately 60–90 seconds for responses. Acknowledge different experiences without correcting personal feelings.

**TRANSITION:**

> Let's examine the promise behind the title of this talk.

**SOURCE:** [T1] — Elder Villanueva, "Prove Me Now Herewith," opening teaching and discussion of expectations.

## 4:00–12:00 — Main Doctrine: The Windows of Heaven (8 minutes)

**SCRIPTURE (invite a quorum member to read verses 10–12 aloud):** [Malachi 3:8–12][S1] — emphasizing the invitation to bring tithes into the storehouse and the promise concerning the windows of heaven.

**SAY (paraphrase of the talk):**

> Elder Villanueva recounts his family's baptism when he was ten years old. His mother, separated from his father, worked to support eight children. Paying a full tithe required considerable faith and sacrifice.
>
> She chose to trust the Lord's promise despite serious financial hardship.

**ASK:**

1. What stands out to you about the Lord's invitation to 'prove me now herewith'?
2. What do you admire about the faith of Elder Villanueva's mother?
3. Why can obedience require more faith when our circumstances are difficult?

**LISTEN:** Invite two or three responses. Avoid presenting tithing as a guarantee of increased income.

**SCRIPTURE:** [Doctrine and Covenants 130:20–21][S2].

**SAY:**

> These verses teach that blessings are connected to divine law. They do not teach that we can set the form, amount, or timing of every blessing.
>
> Obedience is an expression of covenant trust, not a transaction in which we dictate the outcome.

**ASK:**

1. What changes in our relationship with Heavenly Father when we obey because we trust Him, rather than because we expect a particular reward?

**SOURCE:** [S1]; [S2]; [T1] — Elder Villanueva's account of his mother.

## 12:00–20:00 — Second Scripture: Faith before the Blessing (8 minutes)

**SCRIPTURE (invite two readers: one to read verses 12–14 and another to read verses 15–16):** [1 Kings 17:8–16][S3].

**SAY (paraphrase of the talk):**

> Elder Villanueva compares his mother's faith with that of the widow of Zarephath.
>
> During a famine, the widow had only enough flour and oil for what she believed would be her final meal with her son.
>
> Elijah asked her to prepare food for him first, accompanied by a promise from the Lord. She acted in faith, and the promised provision followed.
>
> Elder Villanueva connects this experience with the principle that blessings may follow the trial of our faith.

**ASK:**

1. What do you imagine the widow felt when Elijah asked her to act?
2. What is the difference between trusting the Lord and knowing exactly what He will do?
3. Can you share an experience when you acted in faith before you could see the outcome?

**LISTEN:** Allow approximately four minutes for discussion.

**FOLLOW-UP ASK:**

1. What helped you continue trusting when the outcome remained uncertain?

**SAY:**

> The widow's experience is a witness of God's power and faithfulness. It is not a promise that every faithful family will experience the same material miracle.
>
> Sometimes our greatest blessings are the strength to endure, the guidance to make a difficult decision, or the love of someone who helps us carry a burden.

**TRANSITION:**

> Elder Villanueva gives us a practical way to notice blessings we might otherwise overlook.

**SOURCE:** [S3]; [T1] — Elder Villanueva's comparison of his mother with the widow of Zarephath.

## 20:00–27:00 — Application: Recognizing Subtle Blessings (7 minutes)

**SAY (paraphrase of the talk):**

> Elder Villanueva describes a faithful sister who paid a full tithe but felt discouraged because she had not received an expected promotion or salary increase.
>
> He refers to Elder David A. Bednar's teaching about important but subtle blessings that can be overlooked.
>
> Elder Villanueva then shares his own list, including steady employment, health, preservation of possessions, opportunities for education, gratitude, and guidance from the Holy Ghost.
>
> He encouraged the sister to write her own list of blessings.

**SAY:**

> One powerful lesson here is that we can be grateful for what God has given us while still acknowledging what is difficult or unresolved.
>
> Gratitude does not require us to pretend that every prayer has already been answered as we hoped.

**ASK:**

1. Which quiet blessing have you become more aware of as you have grown older or faced difficult experiences?

**LISTEN:** Invite two responses.

**PERSONAL REFLECTION:**

> Take 30 seconds to think of one blessing you may have overlooked. You do not need to share it.

**ASK:**

1. How might deliberately recognizing blessings change the way we approach obedience, prayer, or family responsibilities?

**WEEKLY INVITATION:**

> This week, I invite each of us to do three things:
>
> 1. Write down three blessings that may have been easy to overlook.
> 2. Prayerfully consider how we are living the law of tithing and what one faithful next step may be.
> 3. Express gratitude to Heavenly Father, and where appropriate, to someone through whom He has blessed us.

**SOURCE:** [T1] — Elder Villanueva's account of the discouraged sister, his personal blessing list, and his invitation to recognize subtle blessings.

## 27:00–30:00 — Closing: Invitation and Testimony (3 minutes)

**ASK:**

1. From today's discussion, what is one thought about Heavenly Father that you want to carry into this week?

**LISTEN:** Take one or two brief responses.

**SAY:**

> May we trust the Lord enough to obey, remain faithful enough to wait, and become attentive enough to recognize His blessings.

**SUGGESTED TESTIMONY — facilitator-created; personalize before delivery:**

> Brethren, I testify that Heavenly Father knows us individually and loves us. I believe His commandments are given for our eternal good.
>
> Jesus Christ is the foundation of our faith. Through Him we can find strength when life is uncertain, peace when answers are delayed, and hope when our circumstances are difficult.
>
> I testify that God's promises are trustworthy, even when His blessings come differently from what we expect.
>
> May we continue to trust Him and recognize His hand in our lives. In the sacred name of Jesus Christ, amen.

**SOURCE:** [T1]. Testimony wording is a facilitator draft.
