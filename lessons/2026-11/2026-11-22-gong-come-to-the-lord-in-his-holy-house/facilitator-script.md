# Facilitator Script — Come to the Lord in His Holy House (30 Minutes)

**Elders Quorum discussion | Sunday, November 22, 2026**  
**General Conference:** October 2026, Saturday Morning Session  
**Speaker:** Elder Gerrit W. Gong, Quorum of the Twelve Apostles  
**Address:** *Come to the Lord in His Holy House*  
**Read / watch the complete official English talk:** https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/general-conference/2026/10/12gong?lang=eng  
**Lesson objective:** Help quorum members understand three doctrinal foundations of temple blessings, reflect on five elements of personal temple culture, and choose one Christ-centered action that will deepen their temple worship this week.

**Central question:** How can we make the house of the Lord a greater source of spiritual safety, healing, and covenant belonging in our lives?

> **Source boundary:** This is an original facilitator script based on Elder Gong's talk, **not** a transcript of his address. Brief talk quotations are credited to the official source; the complete talk is available at the official Church link above. Teaching questions, transitions, and suggested testimony are facilitator-authored. This is an independent study aid, not official Church material.

## Preparation (before quorum begins)

- Read Elder Gong's complete talk, especially the three doctrinal foundations and five elements of personal temple culture.
- Read Moses 1:39 and Alma 34:32–33.
- Prepare the three doctrinal foundations on the board: Eternal Families · Moral Agency · Holy Spirit of Promise.
- Prepare the five temple culture elements on the board or handout.
- Invite one quorum member in advance to read Moses 1:39 and another to read Alma 34:32–33.
- Prepare to welcome experiences without pressuring members to disclose personal family or temple circumstances.
- Keep the discussion focused on Jesus Christ, covenants, personal choice, and practical application.
- Invite someone to offer the closing prayer, following local quorum practice.

## 0:00–4:00 — Opening: The Lord Invites Us Home (4 minutes)

**SAY:**

> Brothers, today we are discussing Elder Gerrit W. Gong's October 2026 General Conference message, Come to the Lord in His Holy House.
>
> We live with many responsibilities: family, work, health, relationships, and the challenges of everyday life. Elder Gong reminds us that amid these pressures, the Lord invites us to His holy house.
>
> Our goal today is not simply to talk about attending the temple more frequently. It is to consider how temple worship can change our hearts, strengthen our relationships, and bring us closer to Jesus Christ.

**SHORT DIRECT QUOTE FROM ELDER GONG:**

> "In a world of commotion, contention, and cares" [T1]

Elder Gong describes the temple as a place of spiritual safety, harmony, and holiness.

**ASK:**

1. What kinds of commotion or cares make it difficult to feel spiritually centered today?

**LISTEN:** Allow two or three brief responses.

**ASK:**

1. How can temple worship help us respond differently to those pressures?

**TRANSITION:**

> To understand why the temple offers these blessings, Elder Gong begins with three doctrinal foundations.

**SOURCE:** [T1] — Elder Gong, "Come to the Lord in His Holy House," opening invitation.

## 4:00–14:00 — Main Doctrine: Three Foundations of Temple Blessings (10 minutes)

**Foundation 1 — God's work is to bring us home**

**SCRIPTURE (invite a quorum member to read aloud):** [Moses 1:39][S1].

**SAY:**

> Heavenly Father's work is centered on our immortality and eternal life. Elder Gong connects that divine purpose to temple ordinances, covenant belonging, and the promise of eternal families.
>
> Temple marriage is intended to last forever. But Elder Gong also reminds us that eternal relationships are nurtured through daily covenant commitment, sacrifice, prayer, scripture study, and service.
>
> The temple points us toward eternity. Our everyday discipleship helps prepare us for it.

**ASK:**

1. What changes when we view our family relationships through the perspective of eternity?

**FOLLOW-UP ASK:**

1. What small daily act strengthens a covenant relationship?

**Foundation 2 — Moral agency and the healing power of Christ**

**SHORT DIRECT QUOTE FROM ELDER GONG:**

> "God will not require a woman or man to be sealed or live in an eternal relationship he or she does not choose." [T1]

**SAY:**

> This is an important principle. Eternal relationships are not based on compulsion. God honors moral agency.
>
> Jesus Christ makes repentance, forgiveness, and change possible. Elder Gong also speaks about healing difficult family relationships through temple service.
>
> That healing does not mean we must ignore harm, abandon appropriate boundaries, or force reconciliation. It means we can turn to Christ for peace while respecting agency and making wise choices.

**ASK:**

1. How does understanding moral agency deepen our appreciation for temple covenants?

**LISTEN:** Allow discussion. Do not ask anyone to describe painful family experiences.

**Foundation 3 — The Holy Spirit of Promise**

**SCRIPTURE:** [Doctrine and Covenants 132:7][S2].

**SAY:**

> Elder Gong teaches that the Holy Spirit of Promise witnesses and confirms ordinances and covenants. These eternal blessings are connected to faithfully honoring our covenants.
>
> The ordinance is sacred. Our continued faithfulness matters.
>
> We do not earn the Savior's grace through perfection. We respond to His grace through faith, repentance, and covenant discipleship.

**ASK:**

1. How can we move from merely remembering that we made covenants to consciously living them?

**TRANSITION:**

> These three foundations explain why temple worship matters. Elder Gong then shows how we can make temple worship part of our personal culture.

**SOURCE:** [S1]; [S2]; [T1] — Elder Gong's three doctrinal foundations.

## 14:00–20:00 — Second Scripture: Do Not Delay Spiritual Peace (6 minutes)

**SCRIPTURE (invite a quorum member to read aloud):** [Alma 34:32–33][S3].

**SHORT DIRECT QUOTE FROM ELDER GONG:**

> "do not procrastinate the day of your repentance until the end" [T1]

**SAY:**

> Amulek teaches that this life is the time to prepare to meet God. Elder Gong applies that urgency to the peace and freedom Jesus Christ offers us now.
>
> We sometimes postpone spiritual growth. We tell ourselves that we will repent when life is quieter, attend the temple when our schedule improves, or seek reconciliation when circumstances are perfect.
>
> But the Savior invites us to begin today.

**ASK:**

1. What spiritual blessing might we be postponing because we are waiting for a more convenient time?

**FOLLOW-UP ASK:**

1. What is the difference between healthy patience with ourselves and unnecessarily delaying repentance or spiritual growth?

**SAY:**

> Christ does not ask us to solve every problem before coming to Him. We can begin with a willing heart and one faithful action.

**TRANSITION:**

> Now let's turn from doctrine to practice. Elder Gong identifies five ways to develop a personal temple culture.

**SOURCE:** [S3]; [T1] — Elder Gong, quoting Alma 34:32.

## 20:00–27:00 — Application: Five Elements of Personal Temple Culture (7 minutes)

**SAY:**

> Personal temple culture is more than how often we attend. It includes our gratitude, preparation, service, reverence, and love for Jesus Christ.

Present the following five elements, paraphrased from Elder Gong's talk.

| Element | Principle | Personal application |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Dedication is a beginning | Spiritual blessings matter more than physical beauty. | Focus on what the temple teaches and promises. |
| 2. Come more often | Temple worship, family names, and ordinance service bless both sides of the veil. | Plan a visit or prepare a family name. |
| 3. Everything testifies of Christ | Jesus Christ is the center of temple worship. | Look for what each ordinance teaches about Him. |
| 4. Practice gratitude | Every temple and its ordinances deserve reverence. | Speak of the temple with love and gratitude. |
| 5. Prepare personally | Where possible, owning and reverently caring for ceremonial temple clothing can express covenant commitment. | Consider a practical step appropriate to your circumstances. |

**SHORT DIRECT QUOTE FROM ELDER GONG:**

> "ordinances are not big or small. Covenants are not big or small." [T1]

**ASK:**

1. Which of these five elements could make the greatest difference in our quorum or families?

**LISTEN:** Invite two or three responses.

**ASK:**

1. What is one change you could make this week to strengthen your personal temple culture?

**PERSONAL REFLECTION:** Allow a few moments of quiet reflection before inviting voluntary responses.

**SAY:**

> Not everyone has the same access, health, schedule, family circumstances, or opportunities. We can each take a meaningful step toward the Lord, even if that step looks different from someone else's.

**SOURCE:** [T1] — Elder Gong's five elements of personal temple culture.

## 27:00–30:00 — Closing: Invitation and Testimony (3 minutes)

**SHORT DIRECT QUOTE FROM ELDER GONG:**

> "He prepares us to come home." [T1]

**WEEKLY INVITATION:**

> Brothers, I invite each of us to choose one action this week that brings us closer to Jesus Christ through His holy house.
>
> It might be attending the temple, scheduling a future visit, preparing a family name, studying a temple-related scripture, expressing gratitude, or taking a step toward greater covenant faithfulness.
>
> Choose something specific. Do it prayerfully. Then notice how the Lord helps you.

**SUGGESTED TESTIMONY — facilitator-created; personalize before delivery:**

> I testify that Heavenly Father loves His children and desires us to return to Him. I believe that Jesus Christ is the center of the Father's plan and the source of our hope, healing, and peace.
>
> I am grateful that the temple reminds us of eternal truths and sacred promises. I believe that as we honor our covenants and seek the Lord in His holy house, He will strengthen us and guide us home.
>
> In the sacred name of Jesus Christ, amen.

Invite the closing prayer as appropriate.

**SOURCE:** [T1]. Testimony wording is a facilitator draft.
