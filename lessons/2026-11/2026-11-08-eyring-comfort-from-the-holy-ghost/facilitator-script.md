## DOCUMENT 1 — FACILITATOR SCRIPT

### Chunk 1 of 4 — Minutes 0–8 · Opening and the limits of self-reliance

ELDERS QUORUM — NOVEMBER 8, 2026

Lesson: Comfort from the Holy Ghost
Speaker: Elder Matthew J. Eyring
Duration: 30 minutes
Purpose: Help brethren recognize the Savior's comfort through the Holy Ghost, respond faithfully to burdens they cannot resolve alone, and become instruments of comfort for others.

0:00–2:00 | Opening — The Question We Cannot Fix

FACILITATOR SAYS:

"Good morning, brethren.

"Today we're discussing Elder Matthew J. Eyring's October 2026 General Conference address, Comfort from the Holy Ghost.

"I want to begin with a question.

"Have you ever faced something in life that you simply could not fix?

"Maybe it was a problem in your family. Maybe a child was struggling. Maybe someone you loved was suffering. Maybe it was an illness, a financial problem, a broken relationship, or the loss of someone dear to you.

"As men, husbands, fathers, brothers, and priesthood holders, we often feel responsible for solving problems.

"We work hard. We make plans. We look for solutions.

"But sooner or later, we encounter something that cannot be solved by determination alone.

"Elder Eyring begins his talk with the experience of a father who learned that his married son had unexpectedly died.

"At that moment, one thought came to the father's mind."

READ ALOUD — EXACT WORDS FROM THE TALK:

“I can’t fix this.”

FACILITATOR CONTINUES:

"Those four words capture something important about mortality.

"Sometimes we cannot fix the circumstances.

"But that does not mean we are abandoned.

"Today I would like us to explore three questions:

"First, what does the Holy Ghost actually do when we suffer?

"Second, how can we receive the Savior's peace when our circumstances do not immediately change?

"And third, how can we become instruments of that same comfort for someone else?

"This is a discussion, not a lecture. Please share whatever experiences or insights you feel comfortable sharing. Nobody needs to disclose a private struggle."

2:00–5:00 | Discussion 1 — When Strength Is Not Enough

FACILITATOR ASKS:

"What are some situations in which a faithful person may do everything right and still be unable to solve the problem?"

PAUSE. Allow several responses.

IF THE ROOM IS QUIET, PROMPT:

"Think about illness, grief, adult children making their own decisions, or relationships that require another person's willingness to change."

AFTER TWO OR THREE RESPONSES, SAY:

"Thank you.

"One thing I appreciate about Elder Eyring's message is that he does not treat suffering as evidence that we have failed.

"He teaches that everyone eventually encounters burdens beyond individual capacity.

"This challenges a misunderstanding of self-reliance.

"Self-reliance is a valuable gospel principle. We should work, prepare, serve, and take responsibility.

"But spiritual self-reliance does not mean independence from God.

"In fact, spiritual maturity includes recognizing when we need the Lord."

SCRIPTURE — INVITE A BROTHER TO READ:

Alma 7:11–12.

AFTER THE READING, SAY:

"Notice that the Savior knows our pains, sicknesses, and infirmities.

"He understands not only what happened to us, but also what it feels like to carry it.

"That is why His comfort is different from merely being told that everything will work out."

5:00–8:00 | Discussion 2 — What Comfort Really Means

FACILITATOR ASKS:

"When we ask Heavenly Father for comfort, what are we actually asking Him to give us?"

ALLOW RESPONSES.

FOLLOW-UP QUESTIONS:

"Is comfort always the removal of the problem?"

"Can we experience peace while a difficult situation remains unresolved?"

FACILITATOR CONTINUES:

"Consider a father praying for a child who has wandered from the gospel.

"He can love that child. He can pray, teach, listen, and keep the relationship open.

"But he cannot force another person to choose faith.

"Or consider someone grieving the death of a loved one.

"Prayer does not undo the death.

"But the Holy Ghost can communicate the reality of the Savior's love, the promise of resurrection, and the assurance that God has not forgotten us.

"Comfort does not always mean that the burden disappears.

"Sometimes it means we are given strength and hope to carry what remains."

TRANSITION:

"That brings us to the central promise of Elder Eyring's talk: Jesus Christ has not left us to face mortality alone."

### Chunk 2 of 4 — Minutes 8–16 · The Comforter and the Savior's peace

8:00–12:00 | Discussion 3 — The Promise of Another Comforter

FACILITATOR SAYS:

"Let's turn to the Savior's own words.

"On the night before His Crucifixion, Jesus prepared His disciples for a time when they would no longer see Him physically.

"He knew they would experience fear, uncertainty, and sorrow.

"But He promised that they would not be left alone."

SCRIPTURE — INVITE A BROTHER TO READ JOHN 14:16–17 (KJV):

"And I will pray the Father, and he shall give you another Comforter, that he may abide with you for ever;

"Even the Spirit of truth; whom the world cannot receive, because it seeth him not, neither knoweth him: but ye know him; for he dwelleth with you, and shall be in you."

FACILITATOR ASKS:

"What stands out to you about the Savior's promise?"

ALLOW RESPONSES.

FOLLOW-UP:

"Why do you think Jesus used the word Comforter?"

AFTER DISCUSSION, SAY:

"The Savior promised more than occasional encouragement.

"He promised the companionship of the Holy Ghost.

"The Holy Ghost testifies of Jesus Christ. He brings truth to our remembrance. He can warn, guide, strengthen, and sanctify us.

"And in moments of grief or fear, He can communicate peace.

"We may receive that peace through prayer, scripture, a priesthood blessing, a quiet spiritual impression, or another person's inspired kindness.

"Elder Eyring specifically recognizes that answers can come through resources, practical tools, and help from other people.

"That matters.

"We should never assume that receiving divine comfort means refusing professional assistance, practical support, or help from family and friends.

"Sometimes the Lord answers our prayers by sending someone who knows how to help."

FACILITATOR ASKS:

"Without sharing anything too personal, when have you experienced comfort through another person who seemed to arrive at the right moment?"

ALLOW ONE OR TWO BRIEF RESPONSES.

TRANSITION:

"The Holy Ghost is not simply helping us feel better. He is helping us come closer to Jesus Christ."

12:00–16:00 | Discussion 4 — Peace That Surpasses Understanding

FACILITATOR SAYS:

"Elder Eyring shares a deeply personal experience.

"Years earlier, he and his wife were expecting twins, but one of their babies died before birth.

"Later, they met another young couple who had suffered a similar loss.

"Because Elder and Sister Eyring had experienced that grief themselves, they could listen and express genuine empathy.

"After their dinner, Elder Eyring gave the grieving father a priesthood blessing.

"The blessing promised peace during that difficult time and throughout his life.

"Think about what that blessing did not promise.

"It did not promise to reverse what had happened.

"It did not promise that the father would never grieve again.

"It promised peace."

SCRIPTURE — INVITE A BROTHER TO READ PHILIPPIANS 4:6–7 (KJV):

"Be careful for nothing; but in every thing by prayer and supplication with thanksgiving let your requests be made known unto God.

"And the peace of God, which passeth all understanding, shall keep your hearts and minds through Christ Jesus."

FACILITATOR ASKS:

"What is the difference between peace that comes from circumstances improving and peace that comes from Jesus Christ?"

PAUSE FOR DISCUSSION.

IF NEEDED, PROMPT:

"One kind of peace says, 'The problem is solved.'

"The other may say, 'The problem is still here, but God is with me.'"

FACILITATOR CONTINUES:

"That distinction can change the way we pray.

"We can certainly ask the Lord to change difficult circumstances.

"But we can also ask Him to change what is happening inside us.

"We might pray:

"'Heavenly Father, I do not know how to solve this. Please help me recognize Thy presence. Help me know what I can do today. Give me the patience to wait for answers that have not yet come.'

"Peace is not the same as pretending that suffering does not exist.

"It is not the suppression of grief, fear, or disappointment.

"It is the sustaining influence of the Savior through the Holy Ghost."

FACILITATOR ASKS:

"How can we become more receptive to that influence during a difficult week?"

INVITE TWO OR THREE PRACTICAL ANSWERS.

TRANSITION:

"Now let's consider why the Savior can offer comfort that reaches beyond our present circumstances."

### Chunk 3 of 4 — Minutes 16–24 · Resurrection, covenants, and ministering

16:00–20:00 | Discussion 5 — The Hope That Changes Everything

FACILITATOR SAYS:

"Elder Eyring teaches that the Savior's work addresses two problems that none of us could ever solve by ourselves.

"First, physical death.

"Second, spiritual death, or separation from God because of sin.

"Through His Resurrection, Jesus Christ overcame physical death.

"Through His Atonement, He made repentance, forgiveness, and reconciliation with God possible.

"This means our ultimate hope is not limited to what can be repaired during mortality."

SCRIPTURE — INVITE A BROTHER TO READ 2 NEPHI 9:10–12.

FACILITATOR CONTINUES:

"Jacob calls death and hell terrible enemies, but rejoices that God has prepared a way of escape.

"Think about the family Elder Eyring described.

"The doctrine of resurrection does not eliminate their grief.

"But it changes the ultimate meaning of their loss.

"Because Jesus Christ lives, death is not the end of the story."

FACILITATOR ASKS:

"How does the reality of the Resurrection change the way we face grief, aging, illness, or the death of someone we love?"

ALLOW DISCUSSION.

FOLLOW-UP:

"How is Christian hope different from simply wishing that something good might happen?"

AFTER RESPONSES, SAY:

"Our hope rests on Jesus Christ and what He has already accomplished.

"We do not know every detail of how our mortal difficulties will be resolved.

"We do know that His Resurrection is real, that His promises are trustworthy, and that His eternal purposes are greater than what we can currently see.

"President Oaks's teachings, quoted by Elder Eyring, emphasize that belief in a literal, universal resurrection changes our perspective on mortal challenges.

"This is why doctrine matters so much in moments of suffering.

"Truth gives our hope a foundation."

20:00–24:00 | Discussion 6 — Becoming Someone Else's Comforter

FACILITATOR SAYS:

"Let's turn from receiving comfort to giving comfort.

"One of the most beautiful details in Elder Eyring's address is that his own family's suffering helped him understand another family's grief.

"He and his wife could sit with that young couple without needing to explain away their pain.

"They could listen.

"They could empathize.

"They could offer genuine love.

"And when the opportunity came, Elder Eyring offered a priesthood blessing.

"This connects directly with our baptismal covenant."

SCRIPTURE — INVITE A BROTHER TO READ MOSIAH 18:8–10.

FACILITATOR ASKS:

"What does it mean, practically, to mourn with those who mourn?"

ALLOW RESPONSES.

FOLLOW-UP QUESTIONS:

"What should we avoid saying to someone who is grieving?"

"How can we help without trying to fix everything?"

AFTER DISCUSSION, SAY:

"Sometimes our instinct is to offer explanations.

"'Everything happens for a reason.'

"'You just need more faith.'

"'At least things aren't worse.'

"Even when we intend kindness, statements like these may leave someone feeling unheard.

"Often a better response is simple.

"'I'm sorry.'

"'I love you.'

"'I'm here.'

"'Would it help if I sat with you?'

"Or we can offer specific practical help: a meal, transportation, childcare, a visit, assistance with a task, or a patient listening ear.

"Ministering is not primarily about having the perfect answer.

"It is about helping another person feel the Savior's love."

FACILITATOR ASKS:

"Think of the brethren or families we minister to. How can we recognize when someone needs comfort rather than advice?"

ALLOW TWO RESPONSES.

FACILITATOR CONTINUES:

"As priesthood holders, we also have opportunities to offer blessings when appropriate and requested.

"But we should remember that priesthood blessings are not a substitute for listening, ongoing care, medical treatment, or practical help.

"We should never make promises on the Lord's behalf that have not been given through revelation.

"Our responsibility is to serve faithfully, seek the Spirit, and trust the Lord."

TRANSITION:

"Let's finish by identifying one specific thing each of us can do this week."

### Chunk 4 of 4 — Minutes 24–30 · Personal application, commitment, and testimony

24:00–27:00 | Discussion 7 — Making Room for the Holy Ghost

FACILITATOR SAYS:

"Elder Eyring concludes by emphasizing that living worthy of the Holy Ghost's constant companionship should be one of our highest priorities.

"That raises an important question.

"What does it look like to make the companionship of the Holy Ghost a priority in ordinary life?"

FACILITATOR ASKS:

"What daily choices help us recognize the Holy Ghost more clearly?"

ALLOW RESPONSES.

IF NECESSARY, PROMPT:

"Consider meaningful prayer, scripture study, repentance, sacrament worship, temple worship, reducing unnecessary distractions, and responding promptly to spiritual impressions."

FACILITATOR CONTINUES:

"I want to add one clarification.

"Faithful disciples can experience anxiety, depression, grief, emotional exhaustion, or spiritual uncertainty.

"These experiences do not automatically mean that a person is unworthy of the Holy Ghost.

"The Savior's invitation is to draw near to Him, not to condemn ourselves for needing help.

"Sometimes we need to repent.

"Sometimes we need to rest.

"Sometimes we need to ask for help.

"And sometimes we need to continue faithfully while the answers remain unclear."

SCRIPTURE — READ DOCTRINE AND COVENANTS 88:63.

FACILITATOR SAYS:

"This promise invites us to draw near to the Lord.

"Notice that drawing near is something we can choose, even when we cannot control our circumstances.

"We may not be able to solve everything today.

"But we can pray today.

"We can repent today.

"We can receive the sacrament with a sincere heart.

"We can forgive, serve, listen, and take one faithful step."

27:00–29:00 | Invitation — A Seven-Day Practice

FACILITATOR SAYS:

"I would like to invite each of us to try a simple practice over the next seven days.

"First, identify one burden you are carrying that you cannot completely control.

"You do not need to share it with anyone.

"Second, bring that burden to Heavenly Father in prayer. Ask for the Holy Ghost to help you recognize the Savior's comfort and to understand your next faithful step.

"Third, spend a few quiet minutes each day reading a scripture about the Savior, listening for spiritual impressions, or reflecting on the blessings you have received.

"Fourth, look for one person who might need comfort.

"Do not assume you need to solve that person's problem.

"Ask how you can help. Listen. Offer a specific act of service.

"Finally, at the end of the week, reflect on what you noticed.

"Perhaps your circumstances changed.

"Perhaps they did not.

"But ask yourself whether you became more aware of the Lord's presence, more willing to trust Him, or more attentive to someone else's needs."

FACILITATOR ASKS:

"Would one or two brethren be willing to share a practice that has helped them feel closer to the Holy Ghost?"

ALLOW TWO SHORT RESPONSES.

29:00–30:00 | Closing Testimony

FACILITATOR SAYS:

"Brethren, I want to close with the Savior's promise in John 14:18."

READ JOHN 14:18 (KJV):

"I will not leave you comfortless: I will come to you."

FACILITATOR CONTINUES:

"That is the promise at the heart of Elder Eyring's message.

"Jesus Christ does not promise that we will never suffer.

"He promises that we do not have to face suffering without Him.

"Through His Atonement, He understands us perfectly.

"Through His Resurrection, He gives us hope beyond death.

"Through the Holy Ghost, He offers peace, guidance, and comfort today.

"And through our covenants, He invites us to carry that comfort to one another.

"I testify that Jesus Christ lives. Heavenly Father knows His children. The Holy Ghost can help us recognize divine love, even when life's problems remain unresolved.

"May we seek that companionship and become better instruments of the Savior's peace.

"In the name of Jesus Christ, amen."

END OF FACILITATOR SCRIPT — 30 MINUTES

Facilitator timing: Each discussion window includes reading, responses, and transitions. When participation is extensive, shorten follow-up prompts rather than the closing invitation. Scriptural readings use the official referenced passages. The exact personal testimony should be spoken sincerely by the facilitator.
